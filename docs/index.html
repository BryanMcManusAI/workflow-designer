A deterministic creative partner for designing ML data workflows

Describe a data workflow you want to build. The partner finds analogous prior workflows, interrogates your design against the failure modes they hit, and works backwards from what “good” means — and every suggestion is grounded in a real prior workflow with a real cost. It's deterministic: no LLM in the loop, nothing it can't trace back to a source. It can't bluff.

Labeling steps

  1. Source & sample — Assemble candidate inputs and sample them to mirror deployment — not just the easy cases.
  2. Write & freeze the guideline — Draft the labeling guideline with worked examples; freeze it before scoring (see conventions).
  3. Qualify & calibrate — Train and gate annotators on a calibration set so everyone shares the bar.
  4. Label — Shown two outputs for one input, pick the better, say how much better and why. Crowd annotators label each item, several per item for redundancy.
    • have multi-annotator-aggregate — Collect N independent labels per item and aggregate (majority / Dawid–Skene); treat disagreement as a noise signal.
      as image-bbox-adjudicated does — redundancy + IoU agreement: N independent boxes must agree above an IoU threshold
  5. Resolve — Measure agreement and adjudicate disagreements into final labels.
    • add tiered-adjudication — Auto-accept high-agreement items; escalate only genuine disagreements to a senior adjudicator who sets gold.
      as image-bbox-adjudicated does — adjudication of disagreements: a senior pass resolves the hard cases
  6. Audit & sign-off — Run the audit strategy below before any data is accepted.

  • add edge-case-guidelines — Grow the guideline from real disagreements: add a boundary example every time two competent labelers split.
    as content-moderation-abuse does — edge-case guidelines: clear guidelines WITH examples that "only just cross the line"

Audit strategy

  • have gold-honeypots — Seed known-answer honeypots invisibly into the stream to score each annotator continuously and catch drift / bad actors.
    as text-preference-rlhf does — honeypots + agreement tracking: known-answer pairs and inter-rater agreement over time
  • add human-audit-sample — Stratified human audit of a sample across the full distribution (not a random skim), on a fixed cadence.
    as corpus-ingestion does — coverage routing: thin signature/modality cells drive the next source pull
  • add cross-family-judges — Re-label a slice with a different model family to separate quality from self-preference.
    as corpus-ingestion does — cross-family extraction on a slice: a second model family re-extracts; framing divergence is flagged
  • add powered-eval-split — Size the dev/test split for the confidence / margin you need before you claim a real difference.
    as audio-transcription-commonvoice does — powered split sizing: dev/test sized for a target confidence / margin

But first — is your good actually good?

The hardest lesson from the detector work: the gold is the thing most likely to be wrong. Each check asks whether “good” is secretly a proxy.

  • sampling_frame — Are you only certifying 'good' on the easy cases? 'Good' on a biased sample isn't good at deployment. probe: stratified audit across the full distribution, not a random sample
  • confounded — Is a spurious cue — length, formatting, author, fluency — standing in for 'good'? Hold it fixed and see if the judgment survives. probe: length/style-control a slice; check 'good' still holds
  • inflation — Does the bar for 'good' creep upward over time? Re-score frozen anchor exemplars and watch the standard drift. probe: re-score fixed anchor exemplars across the campaign
  • unanchored — You have no ground truth — is 'good' just whatever your producer emitted? Without a gold reference you can't tell good from confident-and-wrong. probe: build a small expert gold set; measure producer-vs-gold agreement
